## Mental model

Touch typing moves the keyboard out of your eyes and into muscle
memory: every key belongs to exactly one finger, and every finger
returns to the home row after each reach. Speed is not something you
train directly — it is a side effect of accuracy and an even rhythm.
Every miss you practise is a miss you are learning, so slow and
correct beats fast and sloppy from the first lesson.

## Posture and setup

| Part | Position |
|---|---|
| Back and feet | Neutral spine, feet flat on floor or footrest |
| Elbows | About 90°; set chair height to get it |
| Wrists | Floating, straight line hand–wrist–forearm |
| Monitor | Eyes level with the top third of the screen |
| Breaks | Every 30 minutes: stand, stretch, look away |

Your setup decides how freely your hands can travel. Wrists that rest
heavily on the desk pivot instead of reaching, which shortens your
range and bends the joint. Keep a light touch — modern keys need very
little force, and hammering them adds fatigue without adding speed.

```text
Mid-session check, every few minutes:
  shoulders down?   wrists straight?
  eyes on screen?   fingers curved?
```

> **Gotcha:** Posture decays as you focus. A good start is not a good
> session — set a mental timer to recheck shoulders and wrists.

## Home row

| Finger | Left hand | Right hand |
|---|---|---|
| Index (anchor) | `F` — has a bump | `J` — has a bump |
| Middle | `D` | `K` |
| Ring | `S` | `L` |
| Pinky | `A` | `;` |
| Thumbs | Space | Space |

The home row is where every finger lives between keystrokes. Find it
by touch: the raised bumps on `F` and `J` orient your index fingers
without looking down. Keep fingers slightly curved, as if holding a
tennis ball, so the top and bottom rows are a short reach.

```text
  A   S   D   F  g     h  J   K   L   ;
  p   r   m   i  (i)  (i) i   m   r   p
  left hand ------     ----- right hand
p=pinky r=ring m=middle i=index
```

> **Tip:** If you keep glancing down, cover your hands with a cloth.
> It forces your brain to memorise positions instead of looking them up.

## Finger map

| Finger | Left keys | Right keys |
|---|---|---|
| Pinky | `1 Q A Z`, Tab, Shift | `0 - = P [ ] ; ' /`, Enter |
| Ring | `2 W S X` | `9 O L .` |
| Middle | `3 E D C` | `8 I K ,` |
| Index | `4 5 R T F G V B` | `6 7 Y U H J N M` |
| Thumb | Space | Space |

Each finger owns a diagonal column, and the index fingers own two
columns each — the inner `G`/`H` columns are why beginners mix up
`T`/`Y` and `B`/`N`. Reach from home, press, and return before the
next key. For a capital or a symbol, hold Shift with the pinky of the
**opposite** hand from the key you press.

```text
Capital "J"  -> left pinky Shift + right index J
Capital "A"  -> right pinky Shift + left pinky A
"?"          -> left pinky Shift + right pinky /
```

> **Gotcha:** Holding Shift with the same hand that presses the letter
> twists the wrist and breaks the reach. Always use the opposite pinky.

## Learning order

| Stage | Keys |
|---|---|
| Home row | `F J`, `D K`, `S L`, `A ;`, then `G H` |
| Top row | `E I`, `R U`, `T Y`, `W O`, `Q P` |
| Bottom row | `N M`, `V ,`, `C .` |
| Capitals and punctuation | Shift, then `?` and `!` |
| Numbers | `1 0`, `2 9`, `3 8`, `4 7`, `5 6` |

Add keys in mirrored pairs, one pair per lesson, and drill real words
made only from keys you already own. This is the sequence of TypeFit's
**Touch Typing Foundations** course: 30 lessons, about 90 minutes,
ending in a graduation test. The first lesson targets 95% accuracy at
just 15 WPM — the bar is accuracy, not speed.

```text
Lesson 1 drill:  f j fj jf ff jj f j fj jf
Lesson 6 words:  sad lad flask salad falls
```

> **Note:** TypeFit's follow-up courses, Speed and Accuracy Booster
> and Professional Mastery (symbols and code syntax), are 30 lessons
> each.

## Measuring speed and accuracy

| Metric | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Accuracy | Share of keystrokes right the first time |
| Net speed (WPM) | Correct characters ÷ 5, per minute |
| Raw speed | Every keystroke ÷ 5, per minute |
| Raw − net | Your "error tax" |
| Consistency | How even your pace is; 100% = steady |

A "word" is always five characters, spaces included, so WPM compares
fairly across texts with short or long words. When raw is well above
net, you are typing faster than your accuracy can support.

```text
net WPM = (correct chars / 5) / minutes
raw WPM = (all keystrokes / 5) / minutes

1 min, 330 keystrokes, 300 correct:
  net = 60 WPM   raw = 66 WPM   tax = 6
```

> **Gotcha:** Tools define "net" differently — some count only correct
> characters, others subtract errors per minute from raw. Compare
> numbers within one tool, or your progress chart is noise.

## Accuracy before speed

| Accuracy | What to do |
|---|---|
| Below 95% | Slow down about 10% next round |
| 95–97% | Hold speed, clean up weak keys |
| 97%+ | Now push speed a little |

Coaches agree on one rule above all: reach 97% accuracy before chasing
speed. A miss costs more time than a slightly slower, correct
keystroke, and practising a miss trains it. TypeFit enforces this with
**stop-on-error** — a wrong key flashes and counts as a miss, and
nothing advances until you press the right one, so muscle memory only
ever records correct movements.

```text
Target "the"  typed "thr"
  stop-on-error: caret stays on "e" until e
  free mode:     error is recorded, you move on
```

> **Note:** Professional typing jobs often require 97% accuracy or
> higher — speed without it is time spent on corrections.

## Rhythm and consistency

| Signal | Diagnosis |
|---|---|
| Consistency under 60% | Rhythm is your limiter, not reach |
| Dip with a miss next to it | A key you are unsure of |
| Dip with no miss | A key you are slow to reach |
| Same slow key every round | Drill that key in real words |

Fast typists are not fast because of bursts; they are fast because
they rarely stall. Type to an even beat — slower than your best burst
— so every keystroke takes about the same time. Read TypeFit's speed
curve second by second: the dips tell you which key to drill.

```text
Uneven:  th-e  qu---ick  bro-wn   (stalls)
Even:    t-h-e  q-u-i-c-k  b-r-o-w-n
```

## Daily practice loop

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | Warm up on real text: quotes or common words |
| 2 | Check accuracy, then raw vs net, then consistency |
| 3 | Drill your slowest and most-missed key |
| 4 | Run the next text and look at the delta |
| 5 | Stop at ten minutes; repeat tomorrow |

Typing is a motor skill: frequent short sessions build it better than
occasional long ones. Attach practice to an existing routine — right
after opening your laptop or with your morning coffee — so it needs no
willpower. On busy days five minutes still keeps the streak.
[TypeFit](https://type.fit/) is built around this loop: ten-minute
workouts in the browser, a weak-key drill one click away, and a
per-key confidence heat map on the Progress page.

```text
Weekly goal examples:
  +5 WPM in one month
  95% accuracy at current speed
  10 minutes every weekday
```

> **Note:** Plateaus are normal and often precede a jump. Hold
> accuracy steady through them rather than forcing speed.

## Benchmarks and goals

| Level | Speed |
|---|---|
| Hunt and peck (few fingers) | about 27 WPM |
| Average adult | about 40 WPM |
| Good / productive | 60 WPM and up |
| High | 70 WPM and up |
| Advanced | 100 WPM and up |

Most office work needs 40–60 WPM; data entry often asks 60–80 and
high-volume transcription 80–95. Touch typing at 60+ WPM is roughly
where typing becomes subconscious and your attention goes fully to
the content. Benchmark yourself first, then set a goal one level up.

```text
Developer extras once letters are solid:
  ( ) { } [ ] < > ; : " ' _ = - |
  plus your editor's keyboard shortcuts
```

> **Tip:** Past 50 WPM, TypeFit fades its on-screen keyboard to a
> hint, because by then you should be typing without looking at it.
